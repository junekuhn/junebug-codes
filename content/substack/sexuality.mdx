I don’t think I understand my sexuality. Chances are you don’t either. It’s a very flimsy concept with a dozen definitions, depending on who you’ve read and what background you come from.

There seems to be a few categorizations of how to think about sexuality, and I would consider that (1) Orientation, (2) Expression, and (3) Theoretical Nonsense.

My own experience is very much still in development, even though I’m 30 years old. I “"Thought”” I was straight (can you believe that) until I was 25, when my therapist revealed to me that I am very much able to claim for myself whatever label feels most appropriate, starting with queer. That’s a long part of life to be completely and utterly misinformed about something you should know about from an earlier age. Now I’m a fucking trans dyke with kinks I’m still discovering 5 years after this first major discovery.

Because my brain is still math-pilled from going through engineering school, I think of sexuality sometimes as a vector, requiring both a position and a direction. It is my personal experience that you have to be in a particular position to access certain directions as well. Maybe that implies a vector field. But I’m getting ahead of myself.

Does theory have anything useful to offer?
Obviously this is a loaded question. Of course it does. However, in the case of being trans dyke, maybe not. Transgender sexuality for women especially is incredibly littered with transmisogyny up through today, where there are still little resources that people like me have to access about what’s going on with my body and how to process what I’m processing. For those who are unaware, there’s autogynophilia and people who say “straight with extra steps” to diminish what the complexities of our experiences.

Before getting into the useful bit of theory, I wanted to mention briefly a very influential zine that was shared with me Fucking Trans Women, which defined to me what it means to be no-op, and how to be more comfortable with the body that I have regardless of what surgeries I’m going for. Turns out lesbians can be pretty creative with their bodies and finding pleasure is a fine art.

Theory is useful, sometimes, but for lesbians, as especially trans lesbians, it can be quite useless. What you’d learn in reading anything has to be complimented 10x by being involved with a scene and experiencing things for oneself.

Labels: are they useful?
For the sake of reference, I’m going to talk about Butler for a short paragraph (skip if this would cause you to squirm). I parsed through as much of Gender Trouble as I could this year in hopes of finding something I could use for my PhD and I found a few things that are worth mentioning. Other than the sex/gender distinction being rigorously discussed, Butler takes Foucault’s position (to some degree) that sex (and therefore sexuality) itself is a product of the state and judicial apparatus. But I think I found something even more interesting in the preface:

What about the notion, suggested by Kate Bornstein, that a transsexual cannot be described by the noun of “woman” or “man,” but must be approached through active verbs that attest to the constant transformation which “is” the new identity or, indeed, the “in-betweenness” that puts the being of gendered identity into question? Although some lesbians argue that butches have nothing to do with “being a man,” others insist that their butchness is or was only a route to a desired status

This is really difficult. Isn’t lesbian a sexuality? And therefore doesn’t that make butchness as expression of sexuality? It seems very clear that our language around gender, sex, and sexuality don’t really make much sense outside of the cultures that they come from. I think Bornstein is right, in order to better understand sexuality and/or gender it’s better to think about processes, beings, doings, and actions. Trying to put categories and identities onto things is quite useless sometimes. They’re helpful in early stages, yes, but it’s just Nonsense if you’re in the same position for a while.

Another angle: is lesbian a kind of bisexual?
Let’s get those comments coming. So one place where labels kind of fall apart is the lesbian / bisexual tension within the queer community. Bisexual, by definition, is attraction to two or more genders. Lesbian is non-men attraction to non-men, in SOME cases. If we use these definitions, technically all lesbians could be referred to as bisexuals, as non-binary people are included in the definition of lesbian. A non-binary lesbian being attracted to both women and non-binary people is technically both a lesbian and a bisexual. But, a bisexual man being attracted to both men and women is not a lesbian.

This is why Bornstein’s argument is important. Because in the real world we don’t really consider lesbians as a ‘type’ of bisexual. We’re a culture unto ourselves with our own histories, trajectories, and desires. We define ourselves by our doings, actions, and how we present ourselves in relation to one another. And our relationship with men, is, well, complicated to say the least.

We’re forgetting something
All this talk about identities and labels is completely missing another huge part of sexuality, an individual’s desire (or lack thereof) that’s felt and experienced in the body, regardless of who we’re attracted to. It’s about to get a whole lot more complicated.

I think this is the point where kink starts to make more and more sense to me. Yes, power dynamics are really important to kink, but it really comes down to how. you feel things and what you deem important in sexual experiences. Kink lets you ask for that.

Separating gender from role
I think one of the most important things anyone can do (BIG TAKEAWAY HERE) in regards to sexuality is unpack what Contrapoints calls default heterosexual sadomasochism (DHSM), which is what links roles of gender to roles of sexuality. DHSM is what connects masculinity or femininity to acts of sex, like penetrating or being penetrated, giving or receiving, lover or beloved, etc. You don’t have to be gay to unpack these things either. I think it should be perfectly normal for someone of any sexuality to explore a side of themselves they’re curious about.

One of the biggest things I struggled with, and still do, is finding a sexuality of submissiveness. I like it, I want it, but figuring out how to approach it in a way that works for me is hard. Do I just not do anything until someone becomes dominant by default? How do I invite someone to take command of me? How do I know that somone is really enjoying what they’re doing versus just doing something to make me happy (women do this!!)? I think just because I have less experience with this I have anxiety about it.
