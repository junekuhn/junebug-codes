I recently saw my friend Alicia play at Space Talk, which is a ‘hi-fi listening bar’ with a focus on curation, unique experiences, and very expensive drinks. I absolutely loved it, the lighting was low and muted, the speakers were fancy and bespoke, and the audience seemed very keen to tune their ears towards what was happening in front of them. I also liked that you couldn’t take pictures and phone signal was fairly non-existent. If you wanted to use your phone, you had to step outside, more or less.

I really like listening bars, especially as a musician and someone who cares a lot about sound studies and good curation, but they still feel like a class divide between those who have the money for good clothes and good drinks and those who would look out of place otherwise. If you spent 5 minutes inside, you might think it was ‘swanky’ or ‘snobby’. I don’t know about listening bars in other cities, and there might be better places in say Berlin that do it better, but in London you can feel the class oozing from the walls.

So naturally, this got me thinking. If I could open the listening bar of my dreams? What would that look like? Where would it be? What kind of people would it attract? How could that become a sustainable model for supporting arts, facilitating learning or academic conversations about sound, and creating a safe space for people to listen the way they want to?

I’m really inspired by the London Night Cafe, which looks like this:

A big sofa draped in a blanket and covered in cushions in a dimly lit room


Anyone who’s been to the London Night Cafe can vouch that it’s very unique space, and it’s probably one of the coziest spaces around. Not only that, it’s a very affordable place to go. It’s very queer friendly, neurodivergent-friendly (they have a ball pit!), and generally the best place I can think of to have a social gathering.

So here goes at the criteria for this listening bar, which I might call something like Aural Fixation:

a stimulatory environment akin to the London Night Cafe

a place that's sex positive 👀 and ok with bodies doing their thing with other people doing their thing, whatever that is. of course requires welfare officers who are queer

a place that curates listening experiences, methods of listening, and hosting dj's that do things like what Lucie is doing with Introspective Electronics

a place that facilitates queer ritual akin to what Lunarrr Playgroundz does

a place you could reserve to do psychedelics (in a safe and guided way) with your friends and loved ones

a place to have academic conversations about sound and bodies, as well as sexuality, gender, and cultural studies

a funding model that allows people who come from disadvantaged backgrounds to be able to participate on a regular basis, and conversely hosting expensive things to convince the well off that they're having a quality experience. The London LGBTQ Centre does this well with their private events and board of trustees

Before going into the details on each of these, I’d like to mention that the focus here is a focus on how listening involves the whole body, in whatever the body is doing. Listening bars tend to imagine the listener as a sedentary, cocktail-holding figure with a snazzy dress or vest having a conversation about housing or finance. I want to imagine the listener as someone who can lie down on the floor, bring their mobility aid or seeing dog, play in the ball pit, dance interpretively to strange electronics, fuck in the private room, or receive a massage by their best friend.

I also want to clarify further that I’m interested in going into intersections of listening and erotics over what most people might interpret as “just a nice place to have sex”. Take ASMR, for example. Many people disregard the sexual stuff that comes out of that internet scene as overly heteronormative, overly kinky, or overly porn-focused. It doesn’t have to be. Sure, this is a space where you could definitely exercise your mommy kink from one side or another, but I think what’s ignored is the fact that listening is just another way to experience one’s body. I’m imagining a scenario where you could make out with your date, or explore something closer to tantric practices.

I think this is a nice segue to why this kind of a space would be great for queer ritual. A while ago I was a regular at Lunarrr Playgroundz, whose Instagram bio reads “An experimental coven facilitating ritual-workshops about queer(ing) magic practices, following the phases of the moon 🌘💫”. It was a very special place for me at a very critical moment in my exploration of gender. I can’t thank Byuka enough for facilitating the mindfulness and open-ended practices that went into it. Generally the activities involved short meditations, writing prompts, and tarot readings, but beyond that we connected our bodies to music, visual arts, and storytelling. I’d want Aural Fixations to be the best place to host events like these.

But June, is this just a thing for autistic queer women and gender non-conforming people?

Maybe, but at the same time I think people of all genders and sexualities might have a fleeting interest in some of these topics. Hell, they might even enjoy themselves.

Anyways, the next thing I want to mention is the intersection of listening and a healthy respect for alternate states of consciousness. Listening to music with psychedelic substances, whether that’s LSD, psilocybin, ketamine, etc., greatly alters both the how sound is perceived in the body. It could be used in a ritualistic setting, or it could be a great way to lift anxieties and observe how sound feels.

If all of these topics feel quasi-academic, you’d be right to think so. There’s growing body of work in the field of sound studies, and I believe it’s only begun to form. I’d want this space to be a unique place to have discussions about sounds and bodies in the same place where people can feel comfortable exploring sounds and bodies. I’m very inspired by the writings and doings of Salome Voegelin, Christoph Cox, John Bowers, and Owen Green. The first two are writers and sound studies scholars, the second two are experimental instrument makers. For a quality podcast introduction on sound and philosophy, check out this recording.

Aural Fixations would be a place to critically examine what listening is, and how the listener is expected to listen, and what can be listened to.

Finally, this place would secure a funding model that keeps it affordable for someone living in London on a mere or lackluster salary to be able to be a regular. Grants seems to be an obvious way to get things started, and high-profile private events seem to a good way to keep things moving. Memberships are great, but can be a bit awkward in the way that they introduce a tiered system to a space, granting certain privileges to some and not much to the general public.

With all of this together, I’m putting it out into the ether. Maybe someone reading this will agree this is a good idea and have further ideas on how to make this happen. Maybe someone will steal this idea and make it a reality. Either case is fine with me. I think it’s a good idea and anyone is welcome to take it, because I would enjoy it as either the organizer or the attendee.
