With Valentine’s weekend emerges a either a renewed security in relationship, distaste for our capitalist practices that seek to turn love into product, or a deep longing for what was or what might have been. Through my 20’s I feel like I’ve traversed all three in order, entering a long-term monogamous relationship years ago, entering a new a radical sense of self, and finally processing the trauma and pain that came along with all that.

For years I felt that my prolonged sense of heartbreak was some kind of curse that would haunt me for the rest of my life, and I explore that in parcels, the single for the album that’s coming out soon. In being ghosted by the person who meant the most to me, I felt like I had completely lost my self in losing her. Whatever your position on co-dependency vs interdependency is, the relationship still demonstrates to me the capacity of the heart and how much it’s possible weave a life together with another person.

And ironically enough, after going through such a traumatic experience that left me fairly emotionally damaged for the majority of time I’ve been living in London, I don’t think it’s possible for me to be distrusting or guarded around the people I date now. I find myself throwing myself into relationships, oftentimes without that important conversation of “is this a good idea?”.

Maybe it’s the ADHD, maybe it’s the discovery of who I am now with my relationship styles and gender expressions, maybe it’s the baggage I carry from the past, but does that really matter? I think what matters is that I love how I want to love. Especially when the way that I love is continually ridiculed and scorned, even by queer and leftist communities. I can’t tell you how many times I see some meme joke where “the joke” is polyamorous terminology and how ridiculous it sounds on the surface. None of y’all making these jokes have seriously engaged with the nuances of these terms, nor have y’all been made to feel stupid, naive, or greedy for wanting in the way that you want.

What matters is that I love how I want to love. I reject that I have to carefully plan my love or that every decision has to be though and overthought. I respond to the narrative of “making sure they’re the right person first” with “how do you actually know who they are and what your relationship might be unless you go through some motions?” It’s a little messy, and it’s a little opportunistic at times, but the more I do it the more I find people who are willing to do the same. That’s beautiful.

I do want to talk about an experience I had last night that provides an interesting contrast to the common exclusivity-to-ghosting pipeline. I saw someone for the first time in months after our relationship ended last year. The relationship itself was hardly six months, but damn, the pain is still as intense and almost building from the heartbreaks of the past.

That’s it. There’s so much pain in loving someone, but I think what changes over time is the relationship to the pain. Sometimes I can think of it as an evidence of the relationship on my body, or an effect of the disruption of a mental or body routine. Romanticizing pain is a little 10 years ago, but there’s obviously still a lot to be said for recontextualizing it and processing it through new perspectives.

As we reach the end of winter and enter a new spring, hopefully filled with new love and new joys, I wanted to take a moment and share some thoughts on the pain of it all. ❤️💔❤️‍🩹

— with love
