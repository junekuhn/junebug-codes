ARE YOU JUNE, on the surface, is a generative- and systems-art piece in which collectors choose to prove whether or not they are ‘June’ using Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZK-Proofs). Beyond this, it is a facial feminisation surgery (FFS) fundraiser for the artist, June, and a navigation of a speculative legal identity system. The system, which holds a person’s decisions as a source of truth, rather than a biological extraction of data, is optimistic towards the implementation of ZK-Proofs in validating transgender experience.


Artist Statement: 

Transmisogyny, or the discrimination of transfeminine people distinct from both misogyny and queerphobia, is a well-documented phenomenon that has its roots in patriarchal society (Serano, 2021). A large reason for this discrimination is seen as a lack of trust, and the perceptions of transgender women as deceivers of “biological truth” (Totton, 2018; Billard, 2019). It’s the reflected in the slurs against transgender women as ‘traps’ (Elveljung, 2018) that men claim to fall for. The act of passing, where trans women are often perceived as cis women, for trans women is seen as an act of concession, an affirmation of identity, but in most cases a tool for survival in a world where violence against trans women is so pervasive. The narratives surrounding facial feminisation surgery then, under surface claim of gender affirmation, serve to appease cultures of transmisogyny (Plemons, 2018).
Grappling with these realities, this piece serves to create a system where truth is determined at the moment of interaction, a radical departure from trustless cryptography. In the fictional interaction layer of ARE YOU JUNE, personhood is reduced to a simple yes or no question, leaving each collector which the choice to determine what being June is, and whether they are, in fact, June. Authenticity is later formed, not at the moment of declaration, but through a consistency of decision-making. In this world, we question what it means to deceive.
Validation and proof are routinely questioned for trans women. What’s proof for some is not proof for others. In this system it’s equally important that a collector can prove that they are June, as well as proving that they are Not June. The ZK-Proof is an emerging technology that aids in this conceptual endeavor by concealing private information from both parties in the moment of transaction, reducing identifying information to the bare minimum of necessary data. It could be biological information, social information, medical information, or in this case, the parameters and timestamp of the minted generative artwork that creates an ID photo. The collector, before entering the site, demonstrates proof of Juneness after answering the yes or no question. Based on both the proof and the answer to the question, certain consequences are revealed.
On a lighter note, deception is not something to entirely avoid, and here it’s a playful experience of trying on another identity that may or may not be your own. It’s all over the internet, through anonymity to role playing to actual fraud. We invite collectors to participate in playing with identity what may seem at first to be arbitrary. But as the experience continues it begs to be understood as a system of trust, cryptography, and verification that’s entirely alternative to what currently exists. There’s no biomarkers used in any of this process, and at first it seems like anyone can just be June. But, after the identification process, collectors will realise that there are in fact consequences to a trust-based system, moving reality from biology towards commitment.
The link to trans identity of course, is that you are yourself, until you are not. In which case, a new identity must be created, and you stick with that identity until a new one is necessitated. We both play with assuming another’s identity, that of deception, yet the underlying system we’ve created is one that supports the fluidity, vagueness, and transformation of both biological and physical self. The fear that ‘anyone can be anybody’ is quickly evaporated once the consequences of your actions become apparent. ZK Proofs validate that you are who you say you are, based on the premise that you were who you said you were at the moment of inception.
This system, curiously enough, allows for multiple people to become one person, and for the assumption of multiple identities. This is a feature of ARE YOU JUNE. The question of “Are you June?” does not assume that June is one person, nor that any person can only be June, only that you remain committed about the identity(ies) that you assume. The logic of one-person, one-identity is broken and a world of plurality begins to open (Ansari, 2021; Boticci, 2017). You can be June, and be you once breaking free of Western-colonial conceptions of body.
